DPDP Alignment Assessment — rsdpdp_db

Assessed artefact: dump-rsdpdp_db-202611171147.sql
Assessment scope: Database schema and the sample rows in the supplied MySQL dump only. This report does not assess application logic, API controls, deployed encryption/KMS configuration, access-control grants, S3/object storage, Data Processor contracts, incident procedures, backups, notices themselves, or actual operational practices.
Assessment date: 17 November 2026 (based on the dump timestamp)
Legal framework verified against primary sources: Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023; Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 (G.S.R. 844(E)); and the commencement notification (G.S.R. 843(E)). Rules 3, 5–16, 22 and 23, and the main operative provisions in Sections 3–17, commence eighteen months after 13 November 2025—13 May 2027. This report is therefore a remediation assessment, not a statement of compliance.

Overall conclusion — partially aligned, not DPDP-ready.
The schema demonstrates good awareness of consent, rights, grievance handling, guardian relationships, auditability, maker/checker controls, and planned purging. However, it has material gaps in consent lifecycle design, children’s-data verification, security architecture, biometric isolation, retention/erasure orchestration, Data Processor governance, breach handling, and documentation of lawful basis/purpose limitation. The presence of a “DPDP database” does not itself establish compliance.

This is compliance engineering guidance, not legal advice. Confirm retention, consent wording, biometric processing, and any Section 7 legitimate-use position with qualified Indian counsel.

2. What personal data is present

Under Section 2(t), personal data includes data about an individual who is identifiable by or in relation to the data. A conv_id, card tag, biometric reference, contact detail, role history, or workflow record remains personal data when it can be linked back to a person.

4. Schema-specific observations

4.1 consent_ledger: useful foundation, but not a legally complete consent history

Positive elements

  • Purpose and notice-version references are present.
  • Grant, withdrawal, validity and expiry values are represented.
  • Granted and declined data-field sets are retained.

Material issues

  1. UNIQUE (conv_id, purpose_id) permits only one row per Data Principal and purpose. A later fresh consent cannot be independently recorded after withdrawal.
  2. person.consent_id is a single ambiguous pointer while a person can have multiple purpose-specific consent events. It has no foreign-key constraint.
  3. There is no field for capture channel, affirmative action, consent language/locale, verification method, guardian identity, guardian authority, or withdrawal reason.
  4. The schema does not show whether consent was conditioned on a service, which must be considered under Section 6.

Target design

Use an immutable consent_event table and a derived current-state table:

consent_event
  consent_event_id
  person_ref
  purpose_id
  notice_version_id
  event_type                 GRANTED | WITHDRAWN | EXPIRED | RECONFIRMED
  event_at
  capture_channel            PORTAL | OTP | PAPER | EMAIL | SMS
  consent_scope
  guardian_person_ref        nullable
  guardian_relationship      nullable
  guardian_verification_ref  nullable
  evidence_document_id       nullable
  actor_ref                  nullable
  supersedes_event_id        nullable

consent_current_state
  person_ref
  purpose_id
  current_consent_event_id
  status
  effective_from
  effective_to

4.2 person_biometric_vault: highest-priority remediation

The schema holds fingerprint templates, binary images, face identifiers, image paths, DOB-validation values, and a key reference in one ordinary application database.

Required actions:

  1. Move biometric storage to a separately permissioned biometric vault/schema/database.
  2. Store authenticated ciphertext, algorithm, key reference, nonce/IV and authentication-tag metadata; do not rely on BLOB or VARBINARY as protection.
  3. Link each biometric record to the governing purpose and consent event through validated constraints.
  4. Restrict all reads, exports and decryptions; log them in a tamper-evident access log.
  5. Define a strict retention/purge schedule and test its execution.
  6. Remove raw biometric templates/images from application logs, exports, support tooling and non-essential backups.

4.3 satsang_community: profile over-collection and duplication risk

This table mixes identity, family, address, contact, caste, employment, education, participation, photo, government-ID and document references. This creates broad access and broad erasure risk.

Split it into purpose-bound tables:

  • person_contact;
  • person_address;
  • person_relationship;
  • person_membership_profile;
  • government_identifier;
  • privacy_document_locator.

Do not retain a government ID, photo, qualification proof, or full address simply because a membership record remains active; document the specific purpose, lawful basis and retention need.

4.4 change_history and compliance_audit_log: auditability is good, but audit copies are still PI

change_history.prev_value and new_value, and compliance_audit_log.prev_state/new_state, can retain a second copy of every profile field indefinitely.

Recommended split:

operational_audit_event
  event_id, action, aggregate_ref, actor_ref, from_status, to_status,
  occurred_at, integrity_hash

privacy_audit_detail
  event_id, encrypted_changed_values, encrypted_remarks,
  retention_class, erase_at

A hash chain is useful, but hash_signature CHAR(64) should be a real HMAC or digital-signature scheme with key custody outside the application database—not merely an unhashed or unsalted digest.

4.5 Rights and grievance structures are promising but incomplete

data_principal_rights_request, grievance_ticket, and person_nomination are strong foundations for Sections 11–14.

Add fields or companion tables for:

  • requester identity verification;
  • authorised nominee/guardian verification;
  • systems searched;
  • response/export document reference;
  • correction/erasure action results;
  • legal-retention exception and review date;
  • Data Principal notification evidence;
  • Board escalation/exhaustion status.

The current retain_until fields are not sufficient by themselves. Each retention period needs a documented purpose and, if applicable, a legal-retention authority.

5. Priority remediation plan

Priority 0 — immediate security/risk containment

Action Reason
Restrict access to person_biometric_vault to a dedicated service account and security-admin role. Biometric templates/images present the highest disclosure impact.
Verify that biometric blobs, Aadhaar/national-ID values, document references and JSON payloads are encrypted using managed keys. Current SQL types do not prove encryption.
Stop placing raw PI in generic JSON, audit state, error, and log fields. These fields bypass normal access and erasure controls.
Audit database users, application grants, backup access, object-storage access and export paths. Section 8 / Rule 6 safeguards cannot be evidenced from table definitions.
Create an incident/breach procedure and 72-hour notification workflow. Required for Rule 7 readiness.

Priority 1 — make purpose, consent and child controls defensible

Action Outcome
Create a processing/purpose register linked to purpose_master. Every processing activity has purpose, lawful basis, data categories, recipient, retention and owner.
Replace the one-row consent_ledger design with immutable consent events plus current state. Supports withdrawal and fresh re-consent without losing evidence.
Link minor consent to verified guardian identity, authority, verification method, notice version and evidence. Addresses Section 9 and Rules 10/12.
Review actual notice documents referenced by notice_consent_text against Rule 3. Confirms the database’s versioning mechanism contains compliant content.
Define and implement an adulthood-transition process. Historic guardian consent should not silently become an adult’s continuing consent.

Priority 2 — normalise and protect high-risk profile data

Action Outcome
Split satsang_community into profile, contact, address, relationship, document and government-ID tables. Field-level access, retention and deletion become possible.
Put biometrics in a separate vault. Strong isolation and reduced breach blast radius.
Put login/security data in a separate restricted security schema. Password/IP data is isolated from broad profile access.
Introduce person_ref as a non-semantic internal identifier. Operational systems can work without names, mobile numbers or legacy conv_id.

Priority 3 — implement erasure as a controlled process

Action Outcome
Add erasure_case and erasure_item. A single case tracks all locations holding a Data Principal’s data.
Add immediate suppression controls. Stops profile lookup, card use, document access, exports and integration publication before deletion completes.
Track database records, object-store files/versions, processors, caches, logs, archive tables and backup-expiry obligations. Supports a defensible Section 6(4)/Section 8 erasure process.
Replace retained free-text erasure reasons with controlled codes where feasible. Minimises residual PI after deletion.

Priority 4 — processor, transfer and governance evidence

Action Outcome
Create a processor/DPA register. Supports Section 8(1) and Rule 6(1)(f) processor-security-contract governance.
Create a data-transfer register. Supports Section 16 monitoring and future restriction controls.
Create security-control, DPIA and audit evidence registers. Demonstrates Section 8 / Rule 6 safeguards; supports SDF readiness if designated.
Establish a Data Principal rights/grievance operating procedure. Produces repeatable evidence for Sections 11–14 and Rule 14.

7. Evidence still required before claiming compliance

The schema alone cannot demonstrate compliance. Before a DPDP compliance claim, obtain and review:

  1. The actual notice/consent documents referenced by notice_consent_text.
  2. Screenshots and code paths showing consent capture and withdrawal.
  3. Guardian-verification workflow and evidence for child records.
  4. Encryption, KMS, key rotation, database grants, MFA and logging architecture.
  5. Retention schedule and legal/purpose basis for every major data category.
  6. S3/document-store design, object-version deletion process and backup lifecycle.
  7. Data Processor contracts and sub-processor/transfer inventory under Section 8(2) and Rule 6(1)(f).
  8. Breach response procedure, Board/Data Principal templates and 72-hour test results under Rule 7.
  9. Rights-request and grievance SOPs, including response evidence and escalation handling.
  10. DPIA, DPO and independent-audit governance if the organisation is designated as an SDF under Section 10 and Rule 13.

Final assessment

rsdpdp_db is a promising compliance-oriented schema, especially because it already includes purpose records, notice versions, consent status, rights requests, grievances, nominations, guardian relationships, maker/checker controls, audit hashes and some purge fields.

Its current architecture is nevertheless not sufficient to establish DPDPA compliance. The most material remediation priorities are:

  1. redesign consent as an immutable event history;
  2. build verifiable guardian consent and child-data controls;
  3. isolate and harden biometrics and government identifiers;
  4. replace broad profile/JSON tables with purpose-bound protected stores;
  5. implement central, cross-system suppression and erasure; and
  6. add governance for processors, breaches, security evidence and cross-border transfers.
